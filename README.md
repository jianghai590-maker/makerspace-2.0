# Makerspace 2.0 — Collaborative Online Creative Hub

> Discovery and requirements work for a collaborative online creative platform: understanding stakeholders, mapping needs, and turning qualitative findings into a buildable, governable product plan.

**🔗 Figma:** https://www.figma.com/design/ERMfEDaHzMRvoXWXrVF902/CCT204?node-id=0-1&t=0R7lQjhatGe1abcP-1

## The Challenge

Define the vision, requirements, and governance for "Makerspace 2.0" — an online hub where creators can collaborate, share work, and build together. Starting from an open-ended concept, the team needed a shared, testable vision of what to build and how to run it.

## My Role

Led **discovery and requirements synthesis**: facilitated ideation and prioritization sessions, mapped stakeholders, and translated qualitative findings into concrete delivery artifacts.

## Process

### 1. Discover — Understand the Landscape
- **Stakeholder mapping** — identified key stakeholder groups, their interests, and levels of influence
- **Environmental scanning** — researched comparable platforms to understand conventions and gaps

### 2. Define — Structure the Problem
- **User-journey sketches** — mapped end-to-end journeys for core creator workflows (joining, collaborating, sharing work)
- **Risk register** — surfaced project risks with mitigation strategies, keeping the team honest about unknowns

### 3. Develop — Make It Buildable
- **Ideation facilitation** — ran structured brainstorming and prioritization sessions with the team
- **Backlog items & acceptance criteria** — broke the vision into prioritized, testable work items
- **Low-fidelity prototype** — translated requirements into a clickable prototype for early validation

### 4. Deliver — Make It Governable
- **Governance recommendations** — proposed structures for ongoing platform management, moderation, and evolution

## Key Deliverables

- Stakeholder maps
- Risk register with mitigation strategies
- User-journey sketches for core workflows
- Prioritized product backlog with acceptance criteria
- Low-fidelity prototype
- Governance recommendations

## Outcome

Turned open-ended qualitative findings into a **low-fidelity prototype** backed by a clear requirements package — giving the team a shared, testable vision of what to build and how to run it. The structured backlog meant developers could start building with confidence, and the governance model gave stakeholders a clear picture of long-term sustainability.

## What I Learned

Leading discovery taught me that the hardest part of a project isn't building — it's agreeing on *what* to build. Facilitation skills (running prioritization, managing disagreements, keeping momentum) mattered as much as the artifacts themselves. A good risk register doesn't just list fears; it creates the psychological safety to name unknowns early.
